# Verification standard

## Tiers

| Tier | Meaning | What is required |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Previously unknown archaeological site, confirmed | The archaeologist responsible for the area states in writing that the location is archaeological **and** was not previously known to their records. |
| B | Previously unknown, probable, not yet confirmed | The responsible archaeologist confirms it was not in their records and judges it probably archaeological, but leaves a named alternative open. |
| C | Added to an official record | The authority confirms a new entry in its record or inventory. This does not by itself establish that the feature is ancient or archaeological; several are modern or post-medieval. |
| D | Other documented positive outcome | The authority confirms record absence, places the location on a research list, offers to add it, or comments on an existing record. |

A feature appears once, at its highest tier. A tier-A site that also received a site number is still one site.

## What does not count

- Our own model or detector judgments, however confident.
- Absence from a public map or online register. Authorities often hold much more than they publish.
- A record addition alone (tier C, not A).
- A reply that does not address the location.

## What tier A does not mean

Tier A is a qualified person's assessment, normally from the desk using our LiDAR views and the authority's own
records. It is not excavation, dating or a published report. None of the four tier-A sites has been excavated, and
none has a reported field visit. If an authority later revises an assessment, the change goes in
[history.md](history.md) and the site's tier changes.

## Counting rules

- A barrow field confirmed as one place is counted as one site, not as an estimated number of mounds.
- Known monuments that the detector found again are not discoveries, even when the public register did not show them.
- Corrections lower counts as readily as new evidence raises them.
